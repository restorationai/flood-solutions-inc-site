**TL;DR:** Smoke damage cleanup in Macomb typically costs $300 to $800 for light odor in a single room, and $4,000 to $15,000 when soot and smoke have spread through a whole home. The biggest cost drivers are how far the smoke traveled, whether it's protein or synthetic residue, and whether HVAC ductwork needs cleaning. Most homeowners policies cover smoke damage from a covered fire, including fires that start next door.

If a grease fire stayed in the kitchen but the smoke worked its way through the return vents and left a sour, gray film on surfaces in every room of your Macomb home, you're actually looking at two separate jobs: scrubbing visible soot off hard surfaces, and treating the smoke odor that's soaked into drywall, insulation, and fabric. Those two jobs price out very differently, which is why "smoke damage cleanup" quotes vary so much from one house to the next.

## How Much Does Smoke Damage Cleanup Cost in Macomb, MI?

Most Macomb homeowners pay somewhere between $300 and $15,000 for smoke damage cleanup, with the number driven almost entirely by how far the smoke spread and how deep the soot settled into materials.

A single scorched room with light haze costs the least to clean. A fire that burned for several minutes before containment, or one that started in an attached garage and pushed smoke through the whole HVAC system, pushes costs toward the top of the range because every room, every duct run, and often every piece of fabric in the house needs attention.

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Light smoke odor only, no visible soot, one room | $300 - $800 |
| Moderate soot on walls and ceilings, 2 to 3 rooms, HVAC affected | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Heavy soot (protein or synthetic residue), whole floor | $4,000 - $8,000 |
| Smoke and soot throughout a multi-level home, structure intact | $8,000 - $15,000 |
| Content cleaning and pack-out (furniture, textiles, electronics) | $1,000 - $5,000 |
| HVAC duct cleaning and deodorization after a fire | $450 - $1,200 |

These are typical industry ranges for the Macomb County market, not a quote. Every fire is different, and a licensed restoration company should give you a written scope before any work starts.

## What Factors Drive Smoke Damage Cleanup Costs?

The type of residue matters more than the size of the fire. Protein residue, from a grease or kitchen fire, is nearly invisible but carries an intense, hard-to-remove odor. Synthetic residue, from burning plastics, furniture foam, or electronics, is thick, oily, and corrosive to metal fixtures within hours if it isn't addressed.

Square footage and ceiling height add cost because soot rises and settles on every horizontal surface it passes, not just the room where the fire started. A split-level or two-story home in Macomb or Shelby Township with open stairwells often sees smoke migrate to upper floors even when the fire itself stayed on the main level.

How long the fire burned before it was put out changes the chemistry of the residue. A fire knocked down in two minutes leaves surface soot that wipes off. A smoldering fire that burned for 20 minutes bakes residue into paint, grout, and wood grain, which usually means repainting or refinishing instead of cleaning.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Smoke Damage?

Most standard homeowners policies cover smoke damage because fire is a named peril, and smoke is treated as part of that covered loss. That holds true even when the fire itself started somewhere else, as described by the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance).

That's good news if smoke drifted into your Macomb home from a neighbor's fire or a fire in an attached garage or condo unit. Your own policy typically responds to the damage on your property, since the cause (fire) is a covered peril regardless of where it originated. You may also have a path to the other party's liability coverage, but filing on your own policy first is usually faster and doesn't require proving fault.

Documentation protects the claim either way. Photograph soot patterns before any cleaning starts, keep receipts for temporary lodging if the home isn't livable, and get a written mitigation scope rather than a verbal estimate. Coverage specifics vary by insurer and policy, so confirm smoke and odor remediation limits with your adjuster before work begins.

## What Does Professional Smoke Damage Cleanup Involve?

Professional cleanup follows a sequence built around containing residue first, then treating air and surfaces so odor doesn't come back once the home is closed up again.

1. **Assessment and containment.** A technician identifies residue type (protein vs. synthetic) and seals off affected areas to stop soot from spreading to clean rooms during the work.
2. **Dry and wet cleaning of surfaces.** Dry sponges lift loose soot first; chemical sponges and specialized cleaners follow for residue baked into walls, trim, and ceilings.
3. **HVAC inspection and duct cleaning.** If the furnace ran during or after the fire, smoke particles get pulled through the ductwork and redistributed every time the system cycles. Duct cleaning and filter replacement are standard after any fire where the HVAC was running.
4. **Odor treatment.** Hydroxyl generators, thermal fogging, or ozone treatment (performed when the home is unoccupied) neutralize odor molecules that cleaning alone won't remove.
5. **Content cleaning or pack-out.** Textiles, upholstery, and electronics often need off-site ultrasonic cleaning or specialized deodorization rather than on-site treatment.

This process generally follows the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/), which sets the industry benchmark for how residue is identified and treated.
