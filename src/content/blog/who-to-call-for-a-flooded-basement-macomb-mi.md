If you just walked downstairs to standing water and you're not sure whether to call a plumber, a restoration company, or your insurance agent first, you're not alone. Macomb's flat terrain, clay-heavy soil, and high water table mean basement flooding here usually comes from one of three places: a failed sump pump, a cracked foundation during spring thaw, or a sewer backup after heavy rain. Who you call first depends on which one it is.

## Should You Call a Plumber or a Restoration Company First?

Call a plumber only if water is actively running from a fixture, supply line, or water heater inside your home and you can't stop it yourself at the shutoff valve. A plumber's job is to stop the source. Once the water stops, their job is done.

A restoration crew handles everything after that: pumping out standing water, extracting water from carpet and drywall, setting up air movers and dehumidifiers, and mapping moisture inside the walls and subfloor so nothing gets missed. A plumber typically does not own extraction or drying equipment, and most won't document the loss the way an insurance adjuster expects.

If the water is coming from a sump pump failure, groundwater pushing through foundation cracks, or a sewer or floor drain backup, there's no active leak for a plumber to stop. In those cases, call a restoration crew directly.

## When It's More Than Water: Call 911 or the Utility First

Call 911 or shut off power at the main breaker before anyone enters a flooded basement with exposed outlets, a submerged furnace, or a water heater sitting in standing water. Electricity and standing water don't mix, and smelling gas means you leave the house and call the gas company from outside, not inside.

Once the scene is safe to enter, a restoration crew can take over. If the flooding came from outside, like a storm sewer backing up into the neighborhood or the Clinton River watershed overflowing after a heavy rain event, contact Macomb County's public works department about the broader drainage issue, since that's beyond what any single restoration company can fix on its own.

## What Happens When You Call a Restoration Crew

When you call, expect to be asked how the water got in, how long it's been standing, and whether it's clear, gray, or visibly contaminated. That tells the crew what category of water they're dealing with and what equipment to bring.

Water from a failed sump pump or groundwater seeping through hydrostatic pressure is typically [Category 2 water](https://iicrc.org/s500/), meaning it's contaminated enough that it needs more than a mop and a fan. Water from a sewer or floor drain backup is Category 3, the most contaminated classification, and it calls for antimicrobial treatment and personal protective equipment, not just extraction.

On arrival, a trained crew pumps or extracts standing water, pulls baseboards and cuts affected drywall where needed, places air movers and dehumidifiers, and uses a moisture meter to find water that's traveled behind walls or under flooring. They'll document everything with photos and moisture readings as they go, which matters more than most homeowners realize once the insurance claim starts. [What to Do in the First Hours After a Flooded Basement](/blog/flooded-basement-what-to-do-macomb/) walks through the steps you can take before anyone arrives.

## When Should You Call Your Insurance Company?

Call your insurance company after you've stopped the water and started mitigation, not before. Most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and documenting that you acted quickly protects the claim.

What gets covered depends heavily on the source. A sudden, accidental water loss from inside the home is usually covered. A failed sump pump or a sewer backup is a different story: most standard homeowners policies exclude both unless you've added a water backup (sewer/sump) endorsement. [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Sump Pump Failure in Macomb County?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-sump-pump-failure-macomb/) breaks this down in more detail, and the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) has a plain-language overview of what a standard policy typically covers. True flooding from a river, lake, or storm surge isn't covered by homeowners insurance at all; that requires a separate flood policy through [FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance).

## What to Ask Before the Crew Arrives

Before anyone steps into your basement, ask these questions on the phone:

- What category of water do you think this is, based on what I've described?
- Will you provide a written scope of work before you start?
- Do you document moisture readings and photos for my insurance claim?
- What's included in the initial visit, and is it a free consultation or a paid dispatch?
- Will the same crew handle extraction, drying, and the moisture mapping, or do those get handed off?

A crew that answers these clearly, without dodging the written-scope question, is one that's used to working insurance claims, not just mopping up water.
