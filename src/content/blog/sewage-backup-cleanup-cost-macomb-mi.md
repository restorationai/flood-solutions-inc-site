If sewage just backed up through a floor drain or a basement toilet in your Macomb home, you're probably looking at a brown-tinged puddle spreading across the carpet and wondering two things at once: what this is going to cost, and whether insurance will pay for any of it. A sewage backup isn't the same job as a burst pipe. It's Category 3 water, also called black water, under the [IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) standard, and that classification changes both how the cleanup is done and what it costs.

## How Much Does Sewage Backup Cleanup Cost in Macomb, MI?

Sewage backup cleanup in Macomb typically costs $2,500 to $15,000 or more, with most single-room basement backups landing between $3,500 and $8,000. The range is wide because Category 3 water almost always means removing contaminated materials rather than drying them in place, and how much gets removed depends entirely on how far the sewage spread.

Here's how that typically breaks down:

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Small backup, unfinished basement, bare concrete floor | $2,500 - $4,500 |
| Backup affecting carpet and pad in one finished room | $4,000 - $7,500 |
| Backup requiring flood-cut drywall removal (2-4 feet up the wall) | $6,000 - $10,000 |
| Whole finished basement affected, including contents | $9,000 - $15,000+ |
| Add-on: antimicrobial treatment / fogging | $400 - $1,200 |
| Add-on: unsalvageable contents (disposal + documentation) | $500 - $3,000+ |

These are typical industry ranges for the region, not a quote. A written scope after an on-site inspection is the only reliable way to know what a specific basement will actually cost.

## What Factors Drive the Cost of Sewage Cleanup?

The biggest cost driver is how much material has to come out, not how much water came in. A backup confined to a few square feet of bare concrete costs far less than one that soaked into carpet, pad, drywall, and insulation across a finished rec room.

A few things specifically move the number:

- **Square footage reached.** More affected area means more extraction, more antimicrobial coverage, and more drying equipment running.
- **Finished vs. unfinished space.** Carpet, pad, baseboard, and drywall all get cut out and discarded in a Category 3 loss; bare concrete and exposed framing usually do not.
- **Flood-cut height.** Drywall is typically cut out at least 12-24 inches above the visible waterline (sometimes higher if the cavity wicked moisture), and that cut line adds labor and material cost.
- **Antimicrobial treatment.** Because Category 3 water carries bacteria and pathogens, exposed studs, subfloor, and salvageable surfaces get treated, not just dried.
- **Drying time.** Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers typically run for 3-5 days after extraction and demolition; more days mean more equipment rental cost.
- **Contents affected.** Porous contents (upholstered furniture, cardboard boxes, books) that sat in Category 3 water usually can't be restored and have to be documented and disposed of, which adds to the bill but also strengthens the insurance claim.

A finished basement in Macomb or nearby Clinton Township and Sterling Heights with a sewage backup almost always lands on the higher end of the range, simply because there's more material to remove.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Sewage Backup in Michigan?

Most standard homeowners policies in Michigan exclude damage from sewer and drain backups and sump pump failure unless you added a water backup endorsement to the policy. Without that endorsement, a sewage backup claim is often denied even though the same policy would pay for a burst pipe.

That endorsement is usually inexpensive to add going forward, but it has to be on the policy before the loss happens. Check your declarations page for language like "water backup," "sewer backup," or "sump overflow" coverage, and call your agent if you're not sure. Michigan's [Department of Insurance and Financial Services](https://www.michigan.gov/difs) is also a resource if you want to understand your policy's language or file a complaint about how a claim was handled. The coverage logic is nearly identical to the related scenario covered in [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Sump Pump Failure in Macomb County?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-sump-pump-failure-macomb/)

Document everything before cleanup starts: photos, a list of affected contents, and the source of the backup if it's known (tree roots in the lateral line, a municipal main backup, a failed check valve). That documentation is what supports a claim whether or not the endorsement is in place.

## What Happens During a Sewage Cleanup Job?

A sewage cleanup job in Macomb typically follows the same sequence regardless of who's paying for it:

1. **Assessment and containment.** A technician identifies the category and class of water, isolates the affected area, and sets up containment to keep contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms.
2. **Extraction.** Standing sewage is pumped and extracted first, using equipment rated for contaminated water.
3. **Demolition of contaminated materials.** Carpet, pad, baseboard, and drywall above the flood-cut line are removed and bagged for disposal.
4. **Cleaning and antimicrobial treatment.** Remaining structural surfaces, subfloor, and framing are cleaned and treated to address bacteria and pathogens left behind by Category 3 water.
5. **Drying and monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, with daily moisture readings to confirm the structure has dried to an acceptable standard.
6. **Documentation.** Photos, moisture logs, and a written scope of work are compiled, which is what most insurers require to process a claim.

## What Should You Do First If You Have a Sewage Backup?

The first move is to stop using water in the house (toilets, sinks, the washing machine) so you don't add more sewage to the backup, and keep people and pets away from the affected area. Category 3 water carries real health risk, and it isn't a DIY cleanup job once it's past a small, contained spot.
