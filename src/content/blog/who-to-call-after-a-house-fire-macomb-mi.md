## What order should I call people in after a house fire?

The order that protects your home and your insurance claim is: fire department or fire marshal clearance first, your insurer second, and a restoration company third for board-up and mitigation, before you touch or clean anything inside.

Don't re-enter the structure until the fire department or fire marshal says it's safe. Even a contained fire can leave hidden hot spots in walls, weakened framing, or an unstable roof section. Once you have clearance, call your insurer to open the claim, get a claim number, and ask whether your policy includes Additional Living Expense (ALE) coverage for temporary housing.

Your insurer may suggest a preferred vendor, but you are not required to use anyone but the contractor you choose yourself. Calling a restoration company directly, before the adjuster's first visit, lets you start securing the property and documenting the damage on your own timeline.

## What should you do in the first 24 hours after a house fire?

The first 24 hours matter because soot and water from firefighting efforts both get harder to remove the longer they sit, and a few common instincts actually make the damage worse.

- Don't dry-brush or wipe soot off walls and fabric. Dry wiping grinds it deeper into the surface instead of lifting it off.
- Don't run the HVAC system. A furnace or air handler that's still operating will pull soot through the ductwork and spread it into rooms the fire never reached.
- Photograph every room before anything is moved or cleaned, including closets, cabinets, and the attic if it's accessible. Your adjuster will want this record.
- Discard any food, beverages, and medication that were exposed to heat, smoke, or firefighting chemicals, even if the containers look undamaged.
- Keep every receipt for a hotel, meals, and anything you had to buy because you can't live in the house. Most policies with Additional Living Expense coverage reimburse these costs, but only with documentation.

The [U.S. Fire Administration's guide on what to do after a home fire](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/after-a-fire/) and the [American Red Cross's recovery checklist](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/fire/home-fire-recovery.html) both cover this same first-24-hours window in more depth if you want a second source to walk through with family.

## Does homeowners insurance cover fire damage?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental fire damage to the structure and its attached smoke and soot damage, along with temporary housing costs under Additional Living Expense coverage, but exact limits and deductibles vary by policy.

What the policy actually pays depends on your coverage limits, your deductible, and whether the cause of the fire falls under any exclusion in your specific contract. The [Insurance Information Institute's overview of which disasters homeowners insurance covers](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) is a useful plain-language reference while you're waiting on your adjuster. If you have questions about how a Michigan-licensed insurer is required to handle your claim, Michigan's [Department of Insurance and Financial Services](https://www.michigan.gov/difs) is the state agency that oversees that process.
