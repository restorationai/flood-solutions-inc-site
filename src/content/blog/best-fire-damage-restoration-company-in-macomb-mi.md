## What should you look for in a fire damage restoration company?

The right company responds quickly, is licensed and insured, and can handle the full scope of a fire loss instead of one piece of it. That means structural cleaning, smoke odor removal, content assessment, and the water cleanup that follows firefighting efforts, under one roof.

Before you sign anything, ask whether the company documents the loss with photos and a written scope, whether they board up the structure the same day to stop weather and theft exposure, and whether they coordinate directly with your insurance adjuster. A company that can answer all three without hesitation has likely done this work many times before. The [U.S. Fire Administration's guidance on what to do after a home fire](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/after-a-fire/) is a useful independent checklist to compare any contractor's process against.

## What happens during fire damage restoration in Macomb?

Fire damage restoration generally moves through assessment, securing the property, cleaning, and deodorization, in that order. Professional crews typically follow the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/), which lays out how structural cleaning, content handling, and odor removal should be sequenced.

First, a technician assesses soot type and how far smoke traveled through the HVAC system. Next, [emergency board-up](/services/emergency-board-up/) secures broken windows, damaged doors, or a compromised roof so the property isn't exposed to weather or trespassing overnight. Then comes structural cleaning and [content recovery](/services/content-recovery/) for salvageable belongings, followed by HEPA filtration and deodorization to pull smoke odor out of porous materials. Our [fire damage restoration process](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/) breaks down each phase in more detail, and our [fire damage restoration cost guide for Macomb](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-cost-macomb-mi/) covers what a kitchen fire versus a whole-house fire typically runs.

## Does homeowners insurance cover fire damage restoration?

Most standard homeowners policies cover fire as a named peril, including the resulting smoke damage and the water damage from firefighting efforts, but check your own policy for specifics and any sub-limits on smoke cleanup or temporary housing. The [Insurance Information Institute's guide to which disasters are covered by homeowners insurance](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) is a good independent reference.

Homeowners have a duty to mitigate further damage, so boarding up broken windows and starting cleanup promptly protects the claim rather than waiting on an adjuster's visit. Keep receipts, photograph everything before cleanup starts, and ask any restoration company for a written scope of work you can hand to your adjuster.
