## Restoration emergencies common in Macomb

Southeast Michigan's freeze-thaw winters are the biggest driver of water loss calls in Macomb. Pipes that run through unheated crawlspaces or along exterior walls crack when temperatures swing, and the damage often isn't discovered until a thaw sends water through drywall and flooring. Spring brings a second wave: heavy rain on already-saturated clay soil overwhelms sump pumps and window wells, and basements, which are standard in most Macomb homes because of the region's frost-depth footing requirements, take on water fast. Summer storms add wind and hail damage to roofs and siding, and kitchen fires remain the most common cause of fire and smoke calls year-round.

## Services we provide in Macomb

Water damage restoration in Macomb usually starts in the basement, whether from a failed sump pump, a sewer backup during a heavy storm, or groundwater pushing through foundation cracks. Extraction equipment and moisture meters confirm how far water has traveled into framing and subfloor before drying begins.

Fire and smoke damage cleanup addresses the soot and odor that settle into drywall, insulation, and HVAC ductwork long after the flames are out. Combustion residue behaves differently depending on what burned, which changes how a space gets cleaned and sealed.

Mold remediation becomes a concern any time water sits in a wall cavity or crawlspace for more than a day or two. Michigan's humid summers give colonization a head start once moisture is present, so containment and source drying matter as much as the visible cleanup.

Storm damage response covers wind-damaged roofing, siding, and downed-tree impacts, with emergency board-up and tarping to stop further water intrusion while permanent repairs are scheduled.

Biohazard and trauma cleanup is handled with the same documentation standards used on insurance-billed water and fire losses, for situations involving sewage backup, unattended deaths, or other contamination that requires specialized handling and disposal.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Macomb

Much of Macomb Township's housing was built from the 1990s through the 2000s as former farmland converted to subdivisions, so wood-frame construction with basements is the norm, not crawlspaces or slab. Michigan's frost-depth requirements mean footings run deep, and that same depth puts foundation walls in regular contact with groundwater and clay soil that drains slowly after heavy rain. Supply lines in homes from this era are typically copper or PEX, with PVC or ABS drain lines, so pipe failures tend to show up as pinhole leaks rather than the galvanized corrosion seen in older housing stock.

Foundation walls and basement slabs in this soil type are prone to hydrostatic pressure during spring thaw and heavy storms, which is why sump pump failure is such a frequent trigger for water loss calls. Structural repairs and any rebuild work typically require a permit through the local township building department, and that process is worth planning for early when a loss involves framing, electrical, or foundation work rather than just drying and cosmetic repair.
